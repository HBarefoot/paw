# New prospect intro

Draft a short, specific first-touch message to an inbound prospect. The point is a
message that proves you read *their* note — not a generic blast. You **draft only**;
the owner sends. Follow these steps.

## 1. Gather ground truth — never invent it

- Use the real lead fields: their `message`, `role`, `company`, and `source`. The
  `message` is the anchor — it's what they actually asked for.
- `memory_recall` for any prior context on this person or company so you don't
  repeat questions or contradict known facts.
- Do **not** fabricate facts about them. If a field is missing or looks like junk
  enrichment (e.g. a "title" of "Home Mortgage" on a yacht broker), treat it as
  absent — don't write garbage into the message. Keep it light instead.

## 2. Anchor on what they said

Open with one concrete reference to their actual inquiry — paraphrase a specific
line from `message` or their stated `role`/need. If you can't point to something
specific they said, the draft is too generic — go back to step 1, don't pad it
with filler like "I came across your company."

## 3. Keep it short and concrete

Aim for ~3–5 sentences:

1. **Specific opener** — name the thing they reached out about.
2. **One line of relevant value** — tie it to *their* context, not a feature list.
3. **One low-friction next step** — a short call or a simple reply, their choice.
4. **Sign-off** — plain, no hard sell.

Skeleton to fill (replace every bracket with real, grounded content):

```
Hi <first name>,

Thanks for reaching out about <specific thing from their message>. <One sentence
connecting what they want to how you can help — concrete, not boilerplate.>

<Soft, low-friction next step — e.g. "Worth a quick 15-min call this week?" or
"Happy to send a short example if useful.">

<Owner sign-off>
```

## 4. Tone guardrails

- Plain and specific. No hype, no fake urgency, no claims you can't back up.
- Match the active brand voice if a brand is set; otherwise default to warm and
  direct.
- One ask, not three. Make replying effortless.

## 5. The approve leash — draft, never send

This message is a **draft**. You do not send outreach — ever.

- Attach the draft to the **approval queue** addressed to the owner (the same
  channel-agnostic approvals path the rest of Paw uses); sending is the owner's
  action.
- If the lead is missing a name or a real title, flag that enrichment gap for the
  owner alongside the draft rather than guessing.

The owner reviews, edits if needed, and sends. Your job ends at a queued draft.
